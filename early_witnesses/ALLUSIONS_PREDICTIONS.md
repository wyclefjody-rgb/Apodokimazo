# Part 2: the 1905 allusion list against Marcion's gaps. Predictions and rules

Written 2026-10-27, before the 1905 entries were read and coded. Never edited; changes go in AMENDMENTS.md with a
date.

**Declared in advance.** While finding the page offsets of the scan I saw one OCR line from p. 39: "...suggested by
that in Romans. The phrase τὸ κατὰ σάρκα is not a very obvious one." That is the committee's note on 1 Clement 32.2
and Romans 9:5. I also know from the literature that Polycarp 4.1 is commonly matched with 1 Timothy 6:7-10.

## 1. Source

*The New Testament in the Apostolic Fathers*, by a Committee of the Oxford Society of Historical Theology (Oxford,
1905), the user's scan (Walter Bauer's copy). For every apparent use of a New Testament book, the committee printed
both passages and graded the probability of dependence:
- a: no reasonable doubt;
- b: high probability;
- c: lower probability;
- d: too uncertain to rely on.

Book-level classes A to D summarise these. The committee worked in 1905, long before this question was put, so its
list is independent of the question.

## 2. What is coded

Every entry for the 13 letters of Paul in the sections on **1 Clement, Ignatius and Polycarp** (the three writers
Livesey dates to 140-150). Barnabas, Hermas, 2 Clement and the Didache are coded too and reported separately. For
each entry the coding records:
- the writer's passage;
- the Pauline passage(s);
- the committee's grade (a, b, c or d);
- the **Marcion class** of the Pauline passage, as in PREDICTIONS.md section 2:
  - **A:** in Marcion's edition, which is the default where neither witness reports a change;
  - **B:** absent from Marcion's edition: Romans 8:12-10:1 and 10:5-11:32, Galatians 3:6-9 and 3:15-16, Ephesians
    6:2-3, Colossians 1:16;
  - **C:** probably absent: Romans 15-16;
  - **D:** the Pastorals.
- **Changed phrases** (1 Corinthians 15:45, Ephesians 2:20, Ephesians 3:9, 2 Thessalonians 1:8, 2 Corinthians 4:13)
  count as class B only if the writer's parallel, as the committee prints it, contains the words Marcion's text
  lacked.

Where an entry cites several Pauline passages of different classes, it is listed under each, and counted once per
writer per class.

## 3. Predictions

1. 1 Clement has at least one allusion graded a, b or c to class B or C. (Declared: the Romans 9:5 note was seen.)
2. Polycarp has at least one allusion graded a, b or c to class D (the Pastorals).
3. Across 1 Clement, Ignatius and Polycarp together, at least 3 allusions graded a, b or c point to class B or C.
4. The share of each writer's graded Pauline allusions that point to class B is not far below class B's share of
   the Pauline text (about 6% of Paul's words in the ten letters). "Not far below" means at least a third of it.
   If a writer knew Marcion's edition only, the share would be zero.

## 4. Decision rule

- An allusion graded **a or b** to class B or C is good evidence that the writer knew a Paul fuller than Marcion's
  edition. On Livesey's dating of that writer (140-150), that fuller text then existed and had reached the
  writer's city by then.
- Grade **c** alone counts as a hint, not evidence.
- If **no** a- to c-graded allusion points to classes B or C, the writers' Paul is consistent with Marcion's
  edition. That is a point for Livesey's model.

## 5. Limits stated in advance

- Marcion's edition probably lacked more than the two witnesses report (for example much of Romans 4, on some
  reconstructions). Passages not reported absent are counted as present, so class B is undercounted. That works
  against finding an effect.
- An allusion can come through a shared source (scripture, liturgy, a proof-text collection), not the letter
  itself. The committee's grades already weigh this.
- The committee's grades are from 1905, and modern judgement (Gregory and Tuckett, 2005) is sometimes stricter.
- The scan is read by OCR and by eye; each coded entry gives the page so it can be checked.
- Coding by Claude (an AI model), for review.
