## 1. 2026-10-25: a weakness in L2 found before the Laodiceans text was available

To check that Part 2 runs, the script was tried with a stand-in file: Paul's own Philippians 1:1-20 in the
Vulgate's Latin. (The real Laodiceans text had not been obtained; the stand-in was deleted afterwards.) That genuine
Pauline passage scored at only the 6th percentile of Paul's passages on the argument score. Openings (greeting,
thanksgiving, prayer) argue little, and Laodiceans imitates exactly that kind of passage.
So L2 as registered can mislead. A low score for Laodiceans would not show that the forger lacked Paul's way of
arguing; it could only show that he wrote the kind of passage in which Paul too argues little.
L2 is kept as registered. An exploratory comparison is added (RESULTS.md, L2b): the argument score of the opening
of each of Paul's 13 letters, at Laodiceans's length. Prediction 4 is to be read against L2b. If Laodiceans scores
like Paul's own openings, the test does not support "lacks Paul's argument", whatever L2 says.

## 2. 2026-10-25: what Part 1 does and does not show

Part 1 matches each Latin letter to its own Greek original. Success shows that the features survive *this*
translation. The Vulgate is close and literal, which makes it a best case. It does not show that the features
identify an *author*: two letters by one writer may still differ, and two writers may agree. That would need
letters known to be by one author, compared across languages. (Recorded before the WRITEUP was written, after the
first run of Part 1.)
