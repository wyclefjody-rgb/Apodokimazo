# How often do later writers slip?

Rules written first: PREDICTIONS.md (SHA-256 21f88a62…0835, never edited) and AMENDMENTS.md. Data: topics.txt (the
search terms), CANDIDATES.md (every passage the search found), codes.txt (every candidate coded, with reasons).
Results: RESULTS.md.

## The question

The Daniel-method study found no passage in Paul's core four that fits only a time after 70. Livesey's reply is open:
a careful writer of c. 144 could avoid every slip. So how often do writers who really did write later, in an earlier
figure's voice or about an earlier time, actually slip? And how surprising is Paul's clean record if he was one of
them?

## How it was done

- **The same search on every text.** Each text's Greek was searched for terms tied to things that changed over time
  and can be checked independently:
  - church offices;
  - the name "Christian";
  - Jerusalem and the Temple;
  - circumcision and the synagogue;
  - persons with known dates;
  - written texts and scripture;
  - heresies;
  - rulers;
  - persecution;
  - the expected end.

  Every hit was then read and coded as fitting the setting, a slip (fits only a later time, or gets the setting
  wrong), either, or not datable. Paul's letters went through exactly the same search, so his list was found the same
  way as the controls'.
- **The survivorship trap.** Most later writings we know of were exposed partly by their slips, so measuring slip
  rates on them alone would overstate them. The primary control group (Group A) is therefore made of texts whose late
  date is known from outside evidence, not from slips:
  - the Pastorals (absent from Marcion's edition);
  - the six spurious Ignatian letters (Eusebius knew only seven; the long recension is a 4th-century expansion);
  - the letters of Peter and Clement to James (the Pseudo-Clementine novel).
- **Group B** is reported alongside: 2 Peter and the Acts of John, Thomas and Barnabas.

## Results

| text | gap between setting and writing | datable items | slips | slip rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paul's core four** | - | 16 | **0** | **0%** |
| Pastorals | c. 40-80 years | 11 | 2 | 18% |
| spurious Ignatius | c. 250 years | 18 | 7 | 39% |
| Clementine letters to James | c. 150-250 years | 14 | 5 | 36% |
| 2 Peter | c. 50-90 years | 7 | 2 | 29% |
| Acts of John | c. 60-110 years | 9 | 2 | 22% |
| Acts of Thomas | c. 170-200 years | 8 | **0** | 0% |
| Acts of Barnabas | c. 400 years | 8 | 2 | 25% |

**What the slips look like:**
- **The Pastorals:** a settled church order, and a gospel saying cited as "scripture".
- **Spurious Ignatius:**
  - minor clergy (subdeacons, readers, doorkeepers) first known in 251;
  - the forty-day Lent;
  - the dispute over the date of Easter;
  - Clement as Rome's current "pope".
- **The Clementine letters:** Clement writes to James about Peter's death, but James died in 62, before Peter.
- **2 Peter:** "since the fathers fell asleep"; Paul's letters as scripture.
- **The Acts of John:** Agrippa II stoned by the Jews; Domitian succeeding Vespasian directly, skipping Titus.
- **The Acts of Barnabas:** a written gospel of Matthew in the 50s; bishops of Cyprus.

**How likely Paul's record would be from a later writer** (no slip in his 16 datable items):

| assumption | probability |
|---|---|
| Group A writers share one slip rate (primary) | **0.004** |
| the same, weak slips not counted | 0.008 |
| all control texts share one rate | 0.010 |
| only the controls closest in time (Pastorals, 2 Peter, Acts of John) | 0.031 |
| writers differ: a writer drawn at random from all controls (exploratory, added after the first run) | 0.078 |
| writers differ, closest in time only (exploratory) | 0.047 |

Counting 1 Thessalonians 2:16 as a slip (Paul with one slip in 17 items), the primary figure becomes 0.021.

## What this means

1. **Paul's clean record is unusual for a later writer, but not impossible.** Under the pre-registered measure, a
   writer with Group A's habits would come out as clean as Paul about 4 times in 1,000. Under every variant tried it
   stays under 1 in 10, and it is under 1 in 20 in all but one.
2. **The Acts of Thomas is the warning.** It was written perhaps two centuries after its setting, and it has no slip
   in eight items. It even names Gundaphorus, a real Indian king of the right decades. Careful later writers exist.
   That is why the variants that let writers differ give the largest probabilities (5-8%).
3. **This is the first number on Livesey's escape route.** Her model needs writers who made no slip at all across
   sixteen datable points. Among the seven later writers we can check, only one (the Acts of Thomas) had a perfect
   score, on half as many items. Paul's record is as clean as the cleanest control on twice as many items. That makes
   a 144 date less likely, not impossible.
4. **Paul's items include four "fits the setting" codes not yet reviewed**, but they don't change the slip count. The
   result would change only if review turned one of Paul's items into a slip.

## Predictions against outcomes

| prediction | outcome |
|---|---|
| 1. Group A's pooled slip rate between 15% and 50% | yes: 33% |
| 2. Slip rates rise with the gap | in Group A, yes (Pastorals 18%; the 3rd-4th-century texts 36-39%); in Group B, no (the Acts of Thomas 0% at c. 180 years) |
| 3. P below 0.05 pooled; closest-in-time could exceed it | P = 0.004 pooled; closest in time 0.031, still under 0.05 |
| 4. The later texts that tell Paul's story agree with Acts more often than the undisputed letters do | not tested properly. The Pastorals share only 4 episodes with Acts: they agree on 2 (the persecutions in Antioch, Iconium and Lystra; Timothy's believing mother) and differ on 2 (1 Tim 1:3, Paul leaving Timothy in Ephesus for Macedonia; 2 Tim 4:20, Trophimus left sick at Miletus), 50% against the letters' 37%. Too few to mean anything. The Acts of Paul was not coded (see Limits). |
| 5. Unusually clean, but the closest-in-time comparison too small to be decisive | yes: closest in time rests on 27 items |

## Limits

- **Small numbers.** The controls give 75 datable items in all; the closest-in-time group only 27.
- **Survivorship is reduced, not removed.** Group A's lateness is known from outside evidence, but they are still the
  later texts that survived and were recognized.
- **The search finds only what its terms reach.** For example, it misses 1 Corinthians 15:6 ("most of whom are still
  alive"), and the same blind spots apply to the controls. The terms were fixed before coding (AMENDMENTS.md 2).
- **Coding is judgment,** by Claude (an AI model), with reasons for every item in codes.txt. It needs your review,
  above all the slips in the controls and the "either" codes in Paul.
- **Not yet coded.** 3 Corinthians and the rest of the Acts of Paul (the closest-in-time externally dated control,
  written c. 160-190), and the letters of Paul and Seneca, are not in the kit's Greek. Coding them from translations
  was planned (AMENDMENTS.md 2) but has not been done.
- **Pooling and dependence.** The primary P assumes slips are independent from item to item and that the pooled rate
  applies to Paul's hypothetical writer. The writers-differ variant shows how much that assumption matters.
