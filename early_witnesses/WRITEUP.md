# The earliest witnesses to Paul and Livesey's dates (early_witnesses)

Pre-registered (PREDICTIONS.md, SHA-256 5eb2ae9b…9acb). Results: RESULTS.md. Rules by Claude (an AI model), for
review.

## The question

Livesey dates 1 Clement, Ignatius and Polycarp to about 140-150, which removes them as early witnesses to Paul's
letters. We already know which passages Marcion's own edition lacked (marcion_text Parts B and C), and that his
collection had no Pastoral letters. If these three writers echo passages missing from Marcion's edition, then on her
own dates a fuller, non-Marcionite Paul existed and had spread to Rome, Smyrna and Antioch by about 150.

## What the computer found

The search looked for 4-word sequences shared with Paul and found nowhere in the Greek Old Testament or elsewhere in
the New Testament. It was too blunt (AMENDMENTS.md 1). Most hits outside Marcion's edition were stock phrases ("the
love of God", "our Saviour Jesus Christ"), and the control texts (Didache, Hermas, Barnabas) produced them as often
as the witnesses. With a check added afterwards that each hit contain a rare word:

| text | echoes of Paul in Marcion's edition | of passages Marcion lacked | of the Pastorals |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Clement | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| Ignatius | 5 | 0 | 0 |
| Polycarp | 2 | 0 | **1** |
| Didache (control) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Hermas (control) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Barnabas (control) | 1 | 3 (one passage) | 0 |

- **Polycarp knew 1 Timothy.** "We brought nothing into the world" (Polycarp 4.1) repeats 1 Timothy 6:7 word for
  word, and the word for "we brought in" occurs nowhere else in the New Testament or the Greek Old Testament. The
  Pastorals were not in Marcion's collection.
- **No witness echoes, word for word, a passage that Marcion's edition lacked.** Given how blunt the test is, this
  says little (AMENDMENTS.md 2).
- **One surprise, in a control:** Barnabas 12.4 quotes Isaiah 65:2 in the same changed word order as Romans 10:21
  ("all the day I stretched out my hands"), against the Greek Old Testament. Romans 10:21 is in the stretch that
  Marcion's Romans lacked. Either Barnabas knew Romans 10, or both drew on a common collection of proof texts.

## What this means for Livesey's dates

- **The one firm result is limited.** Polycarp used 1 Timothy. On Livesey's dating of Polycarp (140-150), the
  Pastorals must have been written and reached Smyrna by then. That is not impossible on her model. Some scholars
  date the Pastorals to the 140s, and von Campenhausen even suggested Polycarp wrote them. So this does not refute
  her. It narrows the window: Paul's letters about 144 (her date), the Pastorals added, and both in use in Smyrna
  before Polycarp wrote.
- **The decisive question is not settled here.** Did these writers know Romans 9-11 or Romans 15-16, which
  Marcion's text lacked? Exact matching cannot answer it. Part 2 below tries the 1905 hand-made allusion list.

## Part 2: the 1905 allusion list (ALLUSIONS_PREDICTIONS.md, ALLUSIONS_RESULTS.md)

Exact matching misses allusions, so this part uses the hand-made list of the Oxford committee (*The New Testament
in the Apostolic Fathers*, 1905). The committee printed every apparent use of a New Testament book and graded it,
from a (no reasonable doubt) to d (too uncertain). Each Pauline entry was coded with the committee's grade and the
Marcion class of the passage. There are 196 entries; every one gives its page, so it can be checked against the
scan.

**What the list shows.**
- **Only one allusion to Marcion's gaps is graded above d**: 1 Clement 32.2 on Romans 9:5 ("from them is
  Jesus according to the flesh"), grade c. The committee notes that "the phrase τὸ κατὰ σάρκα is not a very
  obvious one". By the rule set in advance, grade c is a hint, not evidence.
- Every other hit on the gaps is graded d or unclassed: Ignatius on Colossians 1:16 and on Romans 15:29; Polycarp on
  Romans 8:17 and 10:9; Barnabas on Romans 9:7-13 and Colossians 1:16; Hermas on Romans 8:26-27; 2 Clement on
  Romans 9:21.
- **Polycarp knew the Pastorals** (grade b: 1 Timothy 6:7, 10 and 2 Timothy 4:10; six more at grade c). Ignatius
  has five grade-c allusions to them, 1 Clement one (Titus 2:4-5).

**Predictions:** 1 met (1 Clement, on the c-grade Romans 9:5), 2 met (Polycarp and the Pastorals), 3 not met (one
a-c allusion to the gaps, not three), 4 not met (Ignatius's share of class B is 1.6%, below the 2.3% floor; 1
Clement 3.6% and Polycarp 5.4% pass).

**Why this is weaker than it looks, in both directions.** Class B is 6.8% of the ten letters, and nearly all of it
is in Romans. These writers use Romans little: most of Ignatius's Paul is 1 Corinthians and Ephesians. Allowing for
which letters each writer cites (exploratory), the expected number of class-B entries is only about 1.5 per writer
(a-d) and 1 or less (a-c). The counts found (1, 1, 2) are what a writer with the full Paul would show, but
they are also only one or two away from zero. With so few expected, a writer who knew only Marcion's Paul and a
writer who knew the full Paul would look much the same in this list. **The test has too little power to decide.**

There is a further limit. Marcion's gap in Romans 8-11 is inferred mainly from Tertullian's silence (he moves from
Romans 8:11 to 10:2); a grade-c allusion to Romans 9:5 is therefore a hint against a hint.

**What it means for Livesey.** The allusion list neither shows these writers using the parts Marcion lacked nor
shows them confined to Marcion's text. The one robust finding is the old one: Polycarp used the Pastorals, which
Marcion's collection did not contain. That fits the usual dating and also fits a model in which the Pastorals were
written in the 140s against Marcion, so it does not decide between them.

## The outside dates (not computed; for the book)

These are the fixed points any dating of the three writers has to respect:
- **Polycarp died about 155-156.** The Martyrdom dates his death under the proconsul Statius Quadratus, which
  Waddington placed in the 150s. Eusebius's later date of 166-167 is now generally abandoned (Lake's introduction).
  His letter to the Philippians must come before that.
- **Polycarp's letter goes with Ignatius.** In Philippians 13 Polycarp is collecting Ignatius's letters and asks
  for news of him. The two belong to the same moment.
- **Polycarp and Marcion met.** Irenaeus, who knew Polycarp in his youth, says Polycarp visited Rome under Anicetus
  (about 155), turned many from heretics, and called Marcion "the firstborn of Satan" (Against Heresies 3.3.4).
  Irenaeus also calls Polycarp's letter to the Philippians "a very powerful letter".
- **1 Clement was old by about 170.** Dionysius of Corinth wrote to Soter, bishop of Rome about 166/168-174/176,
  that the Corinthians would keep reading Soter's letter as they did "the earlier letter sent through Clement".
  Irenaeus places it in Clement's time (Against Heresies 3.3.3).

**The squeeze.** On Livesey's dates, these things must all fit between about 144 and about 155:
1. Paul's letters are written in Marcion's school.
2. The Pastorals are added.
3. The letters are accepted as the ancient letters of "the blessed and glorious Paul" (Polycarp 3.2).
4. They are quoted as authorities by Polycarp, Marcion's open enemy, who calls him the firstborn of Satan.
5. In Rome, 1 Clement tells the Corinthians to "take up the letter of the blessed Paul", written to them "in the
   beginning of the gospel" (47.1-2).

None of this is impossible. But it asks the anti-Marcionite churches to adopt, within about ten years, letters just
written by Marcion's own school, and to treat them as old. That is the real cost of her dating, and it is a matter
of historical judgement, not of computation.
