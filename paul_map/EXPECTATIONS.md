# The Paul map: what is expected, written before the map is built

Written 2026-10-23, before events.txt was compiled. Never edited; changes go in AMENDMENTS.md with a date.

This is a descriptive map, not a blind test: the main episodes of Paul's life and the classic differences between
his letters and Acts are well known, and are listed here from that knowledge. What is fixed in advance is how each
report is classed and what would count as corroboration, so that the map cannot be bent toward a conclusion
afterwards.

## 1. The question

What does each source say Paul did, where, when and with whom; do independent sources agree; and does anything
outside Christian writings confirm it?

## 2. Sources, by tier

1. The seven undisputed letters: Romans, 1-2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon.
2. The six disputed letters: 2 Thessalonians, Colossians, Ephesians, 1-2 Timothy, Titus.
3. Acts of the Apostles.
4. Christian writers to c. 200 who mention Paul: 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, 2 Peter, Dionysius of Corinth and
   Gaius of Rome (both quoted by Eusebius), Marcion (as Irenaeus and Tertullian report him), Irenaeus, the
   Muratorian list, Tertullian, Clement of Alexandria, the Epistle of the Apostles.
5. Legend: the Acts of Paul (with the Acts of Paul and Thecla, 3 Corinthians and the Martyrdom of Paul), and the
   Acts of Peter where it tells of Paul.
6. Outside evidence: inscriptions, coins and non-Christian writers (Josephus, Suetonius, and others). None is
   expected to name Paul; they are listed only where they confirm a person, office or event in a Paul story.

## 3. How each report is classed

Each row is one source's report of one episode. Its relation to the undisputed letters is one of:
- **letters**: the row is from the undisputed letters (tier 1);
- **agrees**: says the same thing as the letters, or something that fits it without strain;
- **differs**: tells it differently, but both could be true (different detail, emphasis or order);
- **conflicts**: both cannot be true as written;
- **not in letters**: the letters do not mention the episode;
- **setting**: outside evidence that confirms a person, office or event in the episode, not Paul himself.

Corroboration means a report in an **independent** source. Acts may have used the letters (Pervo, Dating Acts,
2006), and later writers certainly knew them, so an agreement between Acts and the letters is marked, but it is
counted as independent only where Acts has details the letters lack.

## 4. What I expect to find

1. No source outside Christian writings names Paul before the late 2nd century. Outside evidence confirms the
   setting only: Aretas IV, Gallio's proconsulship at Corinth (Delphi inscription), Claudius' expulsion of Jews
   from Rome, the governors Felix and Festus, King Agrippa II and Bernice, the high priest Ananias, the Egyptian
   prophet.
2. Every Christian source outside the letters and Acts is later than Paul's traditional death (c. 62-67).
3. The letters and Acts share the same outline: persecutor, called near Damascus, a first visit to Jerusalem,
   work in Syria and Cilicia, a Jerusalem meeting about circumcision with James and Cephas, churches in Galatia,
   Macedonia, Achaia and Asia, the companions Barnabas, Silas/Silvanus, Timothy, Prisca and Aquila and Apollos,
   a last journey to Jerusalem, and Rome.
4. They conflict on several points, the main ones: the number and purpose of Paul's visits to Jerusalem; who
   tried to seize him at Damascus; whether he met the apostles on his first visit; the Jerusalem meeting (private
   or a public council, and whether rules were laid on gentile believers); the circumcision of Timothy; how
   many times he visited Corinth.
5. Acts never says Paul wrote letters and barely mentions the collection; the letters never call him Saul or
   say he came from Tarsus, studied under Gamaliel or was a Roman citizen.
6. The later and legendary tiers add details the earlier ones lack (his appearance, Thecla, his death and burial
   place, a journey to Spain), the pattern by which legend grows.
