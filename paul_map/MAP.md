## Conflicts and differences, in full

### conflicts

- **E09 First visit to Jerusalem as a believer** (Acts, Ac 9:26-30): the disciples fear him; Barnabas brings him "to the apostles"; he goes in and out among them in Jerusalem, preaching boldly, until Hellenists try to kill him. Galatians swears he saw only Cephas and James and stayed unknown by face to the churches of Judea
- **E11 Famine-relief visit to Jerusalem** (Acts, Ac 11:27-30; Ac 12:25): Barnabas and Saul take famine relief from Antioch to the elders in Judea. An extra visit between Ga 1:18 and Ga 2:1 contradicts Galatians, unless Galatians 2 is this visit (as some scholars hold), in which case Acts 15 becomes the problem
- **E14 The Jerusalem meeting** (Acts, Ac 15:1-29): sent by the church of Antioch over circumcision; a public council of apostles and elders; Peter and James speak; a decree binds gentile believers to abstain from idol food, blood, strangled meat and sexual immorality. Paul says they "added nothing" to him (Ga 2:6) and never cites the decree on idol food; Titus is absent from Acts
- **E14 The Jerusalem meeting** (Acts, Ac 21:25): years later James tells Paul about the decree as if it were news to him
- **E34 Rome** (Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.1.1; 3.3.2-3): Peter and Paul preached at Rome and founded the church there, then handed the office of bishop to Linus. Romans is written to an established church Paul has never visited (Ro 1:10-13; Ro 15:20-23)
- **E34 Rome** (Dionysius of Corinth, in Eusebius, Church History 2.25.8): Peter and Paul both planted the church at Corinth and taught together in Italy. In 1 Corinthians Paul planted it and Apollos watered (1Co 3:6)

### differs

- **E05 Persecutor of the church** (Acts, Ac 8:3; Ac 9:1-2; Ac 22:4-5; Ac 26:9-11): ravaged the church in Jerusalem, dragging men and women to prison; got letters from the high priest to arrest believers in the synagogues of Damascus; voted for their deaths. Hard to square with Ga 1:22 (unknown by face in Judea), though not impossible
- **E06 The call** (Acts, Ac 9:3-19; Ac 22:6-16; Ac 26:12-18): a light and a voice on the road to Damascus; blinded three days; healed and baptized by Ananias. Acts' three tellings differ among themselves (his companions hear the voice in 9:7, do not hear it in 22:9)
- **E07 Arabia and return to Damascus** (Acts, Ac 9:19-25): preaches in the synagogues of Damascus "immediately", then "after many days" escapes; Acts has no journey to Arabia
- **E08 Escape from Damascus** (Acts, Ac 9:23-25): "the Jews" plotted to kill him and watched the gates; the disciples let him down over the wall in a basket. Same escape, different pursuers; Aretas is not named
- **E13 Galatia** (Acts, Ac 16:6; Ac 18:23): passes through "the Phrygian and Galatian region" twice; founds no named church there. Whether Paul's "Galatia" means the cities of Acts 13-14 or ethnic Galatia to the north is debated
- **E15 The Antioch incident and the split with Barnabas** (Acts, Ac 15:36-40): Paul and Barnabas part over whether to take John Mark; Acts has no clash with Peter
- **E16 Timothy** (Acts, Ac 16:1-3): Timothy of Lystra, son of a believing Jewish mother and a Greek father; Paul circumcises him "because of the Jews". Galatians refuses circumcision for gentile believers (Ga 2:3; Ga 5:2-3) and asks "if I still preach circumcision, why am I still persecuted?" (Ga 5:11); Timothy, with a Jewish mother, may be a different case
- **E19 Thessalonica and Beroea** (Acts, Ac 17:1-14): three sabbaths in the synagogue; converts are Jews, devout Greeks and leading women; the mob attacks Jason's house; flight to Beroea. Acts implies a few weeks; Philippians says help was sent to him there more than once (Php 4:16)
- **E20 Athens** (Acts, Ac 17:14-16; Ac 18:5): Silas and Timothy stay in Beroea while Paul waits alone at Athens; they rejoin him only at Corinth. In 1 Thessalonians Timothy was with him at Athens and was sent back
- **E23 Ephesus** (Acts, Ac 18:19-21; Ac 19:1-40): two years and more at Ephesus, teaching in the hall of Tyrannus; healing handkerchiefs; the sons of Sceva; magic books burned; the riot of Demetrius the silversmith, from which Paul is kept away. Acts has no imprisonment or mortal danger for Paul at Ephesus
- **E23 Ephesus** (Acts of Paul, Ephesus episode (Hamburg papyrus)): Paul is thrown to a lion in the stadium; the lion, which Paul had baptized, will not harm him: a literal reading of "I fought with beasts at Ephesus"
- **E24 Imprisonments** (Acts, Ac 16:23-40; Ac 21:33-28:31): one night in prison at Philippi before his last arrest, then custody in Jerusalem, Caesarea and Rome; none of the earlier imprisonments of 2 Cor 11:23
- **E26 The Corinthian crisis: letters and visits** (Acts, Ac 18:1-18; Ac 20:2-3): two stays in Greece (the founding, and three months on the last journey); no painful visit between, no tearful letter; Acts never names Titus
- **E36 Appearance, health and manner** (Acts, Ac 14:12; Ac 17:22-31; Ac 26:24): the chief speaker, hailed as Hermes; a polished orator at the Areopagus; "your great learning is driving you mad". Acts' eloquent Paul against the letters' "unskilled in speech" (which may be modesty)
- **E38 Marriage and celibacy** (Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 3.6.53): Paul had a wife, the "yokefellow" of Php 4:3, but did not take her about with him
- **E38 Marriage and celibacy** (Acts of Paul, Acts of Paul and Thecla 5-6; 7-12): Paul preaches "blessed are the continent"; Thecla breaks off her engagement to Thamyris. The letters allow marriage (1Co 7:2, 9, 28)
- **E39 Writing letters** (Acts, Ac 15:23-29): Acts never mentions Paul writing letters; the only letter in Acts is the council's
- **E41 Cephas / Peter** (Acts, Ac 15:7-11): Peter last appears at the council, on Paul's side; Acts never tells of the Antioch clash (Ga 2:11-14)
- **E41 Cephas / Peter** (1 Clement, 5.3-7): Peter and Paul as the two great martyr apostles of "our own generation". The letters show partners who quarrelled
- **E41 Cephas / Peter** (Ignatius, Romans 4.3): "I do not command you as Peter and Paul did: they were apostles, I am a convict"

## Outside evidence

None of these names Paul. Each confirms a person, office or event that a Paul story uses.

- **E08 Escape from Damascus** (Coins, Nabataean coinage; Josephus, Antiquities 18.109-125): Aretas IV ruled the Nabataeans c. 9 BCE - 40 CE (his coins are dated by regnal year); Josephus tells of his war with Herod Antipas c. 36. Nabataean authority in Damascus in the 30s is not attested outside 2 Corinthians and is debated
- **E11 Famine-relief visit to Jerusalem** (Josephus, Antiquities 20.51-53; 20.101): a severe famine in Judea under the governor Tiberius Alexander (c. 46-48), relieved by Queen Helena of Adiabene; matches the famine "under Claudius" of Ac 11:28
- **E12 First journey: Cyprus and southern Asia Minor** (Inscriptions, ILS 5926 (CIL VI 31545); the Soli inscription): a Lucius Sergius Paullus was one of the curators of the Tiber banks appointed in 47, and later senators of the name came from Pisidian Antioch; an inscription from Soli on Cyprus names a proconsul Paulus but may be later. Whether any of them is Acts' proconsul is disputed (Mitford: "at best a conjecture"). Cyprus was governed by a proconsul, as Acts says
- **E19 Thessalonica and Beroea** (Inscriptions, inscriptions of Thessalonica): the city's magistrates are called "politarchs" in its inscriptions, the title Acts uses (Ac 17:6, 8)
- **E21 Corinth: founding the church** (Delphi inscription, Fouilles de Delphes III 4:286; SIG3 801 D): a letter of Claudius, dated by his 26th acclamation as emperor to 52, names "Junius Gallio ... proconsul" of Achaia; his year of office was 51-52 or 52-53. The main anchor for Paul's chronology
- **E21 Corinth: founding the church** (Suetonius, Claudius 25.4): Claudius "expelled from Rome the Jews, who were constantly rioting at the instigation of Chrestus"; Orosius (5th century, 7.6.15) dates it to 49
- **E21 Corinth: founding the church** (Corinth inscription, Erastus pavement inscription): "Erastus, in return for his aedileship, laid [this pavement] at his own expense"; both its date and whether he is the "city treasurer" of Ro 16:23 are disputed
- **E23 Ephesus** (Inscriptions, inscriptions of Ephesus): the cult of Artemis, silversmiths' guilds, the great theatre and the provincial "Asiarchs" (Ac 19:31) are all attested at Ephesus
- **E31 Arrest in the Temple** (Temple warning inscriptions, Josephus, War 5.193-194; Antiquities 15.417): notices on the Temple barrier forbade gentiles to pass on pain of death; two have been found (1871, 1935)
- **E31 Arrest in the Temple** (Josephus, War 2.261-263; Antiquities 20.169-172): an Egyptian false prophet under Felix led a crowd (30,000 in War) to the Mount of Olives and escaped; the numbers differ from Acts
- **E31 Arrest in the Temple** (Josephus, Antiquities 20.103, 131, 205-213): Ananias son of Nedebaeus, high priest c. 47-59
- **E32 Caesarea: Felix, Festus, Agrippa** (Tacitus, Annals 12.54; Histories 5.9; Josephus, Antiquities 20.137-144): Felix, a freedman, governor of Judea c. 52-60, married Drusilla, sister of Agrippa II
- **E32 Caesarea: Felix, Festus, Agrippa** (Josephus, Antiquities 20.182-197; War 2.271): Porcius Festus, governor c. 60-62, died in office; Agrippa II and his sister Bernice (also Tacitus, Histories 2.2)
- **E33 Shipwrecks; the voyage to Rome** (Inscriptions, CIG 5754 and other inscriptions of Malta and Gozo): "first" (protos) is attested as an official title in the Maltese islands
