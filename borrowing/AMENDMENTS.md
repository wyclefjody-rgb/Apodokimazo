## 1. 2026-10-24: B5 made exact (written into borrowing.py before the first run)

B5 compares 1 Peter 2:13-17 (5 verses) with "every window of 7 verses". The code counts B5 as met if the
highest-scoring 7-verse window contains at least 3 of the verses 2:13-17. For Mark (B4) the window is exactly
12:13-17.

## 2. 2026-10-24: after the first run. The calibration failed (B1)

Only one of the three known-borrowing pairs (Ephesians and Colossians) passed the rule. 2 Peter and Jude scored at
the 99th percentile on rare shared words but only the 80th on shared strings. 2 Peter rewrites Jude in its own
words, keeping Jude's rare vocabulary (Balaam, Gomorrah, "waterless", "scoffers", "deepest darkness"), not its
sentences. 2 and 1 Thessalonians passed on strings (100th) but not on rare words (88th).
As PREDICTIONS.md section 4 says, the rule is therefore too weak to clear anyone, and its verdicts are reported
as such. Two lessons are recorded for any future version, not applied here:
- Borrowing can look like 2 Peter and Jude (paraphrase: rare words, few strings) as well as like Ephesians and
  Colossians (strings). A single string threshold misses the first kind.
- Short control letters (Philemon especially) score high on strings because of shared letter formulas ("grace
  to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ"). A version that removes the opening and closing
  formulas before counting would be fairer.

## 3. 2026-10-24: note on S5 (Marcion's gospel)

Mark and Matthew also join "blood" and "covenant" ("this is my blood of the covenant"), so "covenant" plus "blood"
alone does not separate the Luke/Paul form. What does: Tertullian's chapter starts from Luke 22:15 (Jesus "eagerly
desired to eat this Passover"), which is only in Luke, and reports a cup with "the covenant sealed in his blood"
*after* the bread. In Luke that cup saying exists only in the longer text (22:20). S5 is counted as met on that
basis, with the caution that Tertullian paraphrases.
