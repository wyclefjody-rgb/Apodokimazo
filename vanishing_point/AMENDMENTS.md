## 1. 2026-10-23: Ascension of Isaiah added as a control (before Part B was coded)

Added at the user's request. The Ascension of Isaiah is an early Christian apocalypse whose Christian sections
(the "Testament of Hezekiah", 3:13-4:22, and the "Vision of Isaiah", chapters 6-11) are usually dated between
the late 1st and the 2nd century. It survives complete only in Ethiopic (with Latin, Slavonic and Coptic parts and
Greek fragments), so it cannot enter Part A or any of the kit's Greek word counts. It is coded in Part B from the
published translations and scholarship, as a text whose own vanishing point the method should be able to find
(its Nero-like antichrist in 4:2-3 and its claim in 4:13 that some who saw the Lord will still be alive).

## 2. 2026-10-23: correction to a limit in PREDICTIONS.md section 6 (before Part B was coded)

Section 6 says 1 Clement and Josephus "also speak of Temple worship in the present tense after 70". That is
disputed: the present tenses in Josephus (Antiquities 4.241-242) occur inside a speech given to Moses, 1 Clement
41.2 is itself dated by some before 70 because of it, and the clearest unmarked case (Diognetus 3) is of uncertain
date (Jimmy Akin, "References to the Temple operating after A.D. 70?", 2015, reviewing W. L. Lane's examples). The
coding rule is unchanged: present-tense mentions of the Temple are coded "either", the cautious choice.

## 4. 2026-10-24: item 15 (Marcion) corrected

Item 15 said Marcion "rejected" Israel's scriptures. That overstates it. On the reading Litwa defends, Marcion
accepted them as the true scriptures of the Creator, the God of Israel, and denied only that they speak of the
Father of Jesus. Item 15 also leaned on Romans 9-11, but Tertullian (Against Marcion 5.14) says Marcion's Romans
jumped from 8:11 to 10:2 over "a very wide gap of cut-out scripture" and that the material before 11:33 had been
erased. So those chapters are no evidence about what Marcion's own text contained.
The question item 15 raised is now tested directly in marcion_text (Part B, pre-registered 2026-10-24): which
passages Tertullian says stood in Marcion's edition, and which of those go against Marcion's theology on their
face. Item 15 stays "(provenance, not date)" and is not counted in the date tally either way.
