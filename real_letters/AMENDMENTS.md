## 1. 2026-10-27: decisions while coding, and one exploratory table

- **Pliny's sample includes Trajan's replies** (10.3b, 10.7, 10.9 and so on). They are genuine letters of the same
  correspondence; the letters numbered 3a/3b and 17a/17b in the Perseus edition count under 3 and 17.
- **Editors' date headings** in the Perseus Cicero ("Scr. Romae ... a. 689 (65)") are modern and are left out of
  the word count and the coding.
- **Correct statements about the past** (Seneca on Pompey's death, Cato, Atticus' family) are coded "either":
  they would fit a later writer too, but a wrong one would be a slip, as in slip_rate (the Acts of John's Agrippa).
- **Three citations were dropped while checking the codes, before the script was run,** because I could not
  confirm them: a Dio reference for Trajan's birthday (replaced by the Fasti), a claim that Plutarch gives the vote
  count at Clodius' trial, and a source for the year of Catulus' death (that item was removed; the Cincian law
  stayed, coded "either").
- **Exploratory, added after the first run:** the likelihood ratio is also shown against all eight later writings
  of slip_rate plus 3 Corinthians (124 to 1) and against those closest in time (79 to 1). The registered ratio
  (347 to 1) uses Group A plus 3 Corinthians, as section 4 says.

## 2. 2026-10-27: the main weakness, stated plainly

The coder knew which letters were genuine and which were not. A blind recoding (a second reader, or the same items
with the authors' names hidden) is the proper check, and has not been done.
